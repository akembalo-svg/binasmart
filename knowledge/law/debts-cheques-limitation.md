# Unpaid debts and cheques — Civil Code limitation period, Commercial Code cheque rules, Criminal Code Art. 693 — ያልተከፈለ ዕዳና ቼክ

## What these rules say (in brief)

- Civil Code Art. 1845 — unless otherwise provided by law, actions for the performance of a contract (for example repayment of a loan), for non-performance, or for invalidation of a contract are barred if not brought within ten years. Art. 1846 — the ten years run from the day the obligation became due.
- Commercial Code Art. 827 — a cheque must contain an unconditional order to pay a sum of money, the name of the paying bank (drawee), the place of payment, the date and place of drawing, and the drawer's signature. Art. 830 — a cheque may only be issued if the drawer has funds with the bank.
- Art. 854 — a cheque is payable at sight. Art. 855 — it must be presented for payment within six months of its date.
- Arts. 868–869 — if a cheque presented in time is not paid, the holder can claim from the drawer and endorsers once non-payment is evidenced by a protest or by a dated declaration of the bank written on the cheque, made before the presentment period ends; notice of non-payment is given within four working days.
- Art. 881 — the holder's action against the drawer and endorsers is barred six months after the presentment period ends; the action against the bank after three years.
- Criminal Code Art. 693 — whoever intentionally draws a cheque without cover, or knowing there will not be full cover when it is presented, is punishable with simple imprisonment or, in serious cases, rigorous imprisonment of up to ten years, and a fine; if done negligently, a fine or simple imprisonment of up to one year.
