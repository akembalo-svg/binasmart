# Ethiopia's Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 1389/2025

Federal Negarit Gazette, 31st Year No. 59, Addis Ababa, **14 August 2025** (ነሐሴ ፰ ቀን ፪ሺ፲፯ ዓ.ም. = ነሐሴ 8 ቀን 2017 ዓ.ም.), pages 19016–19089. Enacted under Article 55(1) of the Constitution. Signed at Addis Ababa on 14 August 2025 by **Taye Atske Selassie, President of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia**. 89 articles in seven Parts. The Amharic short title cites the proclamation as **አዋጅ ቁጥር ፩ሺ፫፻፹፱/፪ሺ፲፯ (1389/2017 E.C.)**; the English short title is "Ethiopia's Overseas Employment Proclamation No 1389/2025". They are the same law.

## Date it came into force — one date, not three

**Article 89:** "This Proclamation shall enter into force on the date of publication in the Federal Negarit Gazette."

It was published in the Federal Negarit Gazette on **14 August 2025 = ነሐሴ 8 ቀን 2017 ዓ.ም.** There is no separate, later commencement date, no waiting period and no ministerial order needed to bring it into force. Publication date and entry-into-force date are the same day: **14 August 2025**.

## What it replaces

- **Article 86(1)** — Overseas Employment Proclamation **No. 923/2016** and its amendment Proclamation **No. 1246/2021** are repealed.
- **Article 86(2)** — no other law, regulation, directive or practice applies where it is inconsistent with this proclamation.
- **Article 87** — regulations and directives issued under 923/2016 and 1246/2021 **stay in force** so far as they are not inconsistent with this proclamation, until replaced by regulations and directives issued under it.
- **Article 88 (transition)** — a labour dispute about an overseas worker already pending before a competent organ when the proclamation came into force is settled under the old law and procedure. An agency licensed under 923/2016 that wants to keep operating **must obtain a new licence within three months from the effective date**; if it does not, its old licence becomes null and void. Revocation of an agency's licence does not release it from claims raised by workers it deployed.

## Who the law applies to (Arts. 2–3)

Article 3: it applies to the overseas employment of Ethiopians through **public employment organs, through an Agency, or by direct employment**.

Definitions that carry the rest of the law (Art. 2):

- **Ministry** — the Ministry of Labor and Skills.
- **Agency** — a non-governmental entity licensed by the Ministry to supply a worker to an employer for overseas employment.
- **Licence** — a deployment or competence certificate issued by the Ministry to eligible agencies.
- **Worker** — a person in a contractual relationship with an employer or agency holding **skilled, semi-skilled or experience-based** qualifications; includes a person going through the overseas employment process.
- **Direct employment** — employment arranged between employer and worker with no government organ and no agency involved.
- **Labor Attaché** — an officer assigned by the Ministry, in consultation with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, to a receiving country, to monitor and support the protection of the rights, safety and dignity of Ethiopians employed overseas.
- **Bilateral Agreement** — between the FDRE and the receiving country. **Memorandum of Understanding** — between the FDRE government and a receiving-country government, or an employer in the receiving country, or between a legal agency in Ethiopia and an employer in the receiving country; it is not legally binding and shows an intention to cooperate.
- **Domestic Work** — non-profit work for the household consumption of the employer and their family: cooking, cleaning, babysitting, security, driving, gardening and similar tasks.
- **Association of Agencies** — an association established by **more than three hundred** agencies. **Federation of Agencies** — formed by agency associations.
- **One Stop Service** — a centre where citizens register for overseas employment and receive related services.
- **Contract of Employment** — a **written** agreement between an employer and a worker.

## Who may be sent abroad — skills, education, competency, age

**Three categories of worker may be deployed (Arts. 12–14):**

- **Art. 12, skilled worker** — has the educational qualifications, specialised sectoral knowledge and skills and the professional and work experience the position requires, **and** holds an occupational competency certificate from an accredited assessment centre as needed for the field.
- **Art. 13, semi-skilled worker** — partly meets the educational qualifications, has partial sectoral knowledge gained through education or experience, and the post does not demand high professional skill; **and** holds an occupational competency certificate as needed.
- **Art. 14, experience-based worker** — has knowledge and skills acquired through experience, where the post requires no education and involves routine, largely manual tasks; **and** holds an occupational competency certificate as needed.

**Education — Art. 15:** any worker going abroad shall acquire the knowledge or skills necessary to perform the work **and shall at least be able to read and comprehend the contract document prepared in a language he understands.** That literacy test is the floor; the proclamation sets no minimum grade or certificate level.

**Competency — Art. 16:** the worker must hold an occupational competency certificate issued by a relevant educational institution or assessment centre. A returnee going back to the **same** field needs **refresher training** plus a certificate (16(2)); a returnee moving to a **different** field needs **new training** plus a certificate (16(3)).

**Age — 18 years.** The proclamation contains no standalone minimum-age article. The rule appears as an offence: **Art. 50(4)(d)** — "deployment of a worker who is below the age of eighteen years" is a ground for **revocation of the agency's licence**. So the effective minimum age for overseas employment is **18**. (The MoLS implementation directive repeats it at the registration counter: registration at a one-stop centre requires the applicant to be 18 or over — see the companion file on that directive.)

## Pre-departure — the sequence the law actually lays down

1. **Register through the Labor Market Information System (LMIS).** Art. 5 defines Ethiopia's LMIS as the digital system for collecting, storing, managing and updating all data on overseas employment — workers' personal data, employment contracts, particulars of agencies, other service providers and grievances. **Art. 5(3): every overseas employment service must pass through the LMIS and be duly registered.** Art. 44(1): recruitment of any worker wanting to be deployed abroad shall be conducted **only** through the centralized LMIS, beginning at a one-stop centre. Art. 83(3): recruitment or deployment done outside the LMIS is **prohibited**.
2. **One Stop Service Centre — Art. 9:** registers citizens seeking overseas employment and provides related services.
3. **Skills training — Art. 10:** public TVET institutions and licensed private training institutions deliver the training; the Ministry supports, follows up and monitors it, sets training fees on the basis of study, and issues a directive on assessment of workers' skills.
4. **Occupational competency assessment — Art. 11:** assessment centres assess those who completed training, notify the competence level through the information-exchange system, and assess knowledge and skills acquired by any means.
5. **Medical examination — Art. 17:** carried out **exclusively** at health institutions jointly selected by the Federal Ministry of Health and, as necessary, the countries of destination. It is done **only once it is established that the worker meets the employer's other requirements** (17(2)). The Ministry and the Ministry of Health issue a joint directive on selecting the facilities and on the examination process (17(3)).
6. **Travel documents — Art. 18:** presented to the Ministry by the agency or the worker as appropriate; the Ministry works with the relevant bodies to complete them.
7. **Contract signed and approved — Art. 45** (below).
8. **Work identification number.** Art. 73(1)(c) requires a worker returning on leave to obtain a **work identification number** if not already obtained — the proclamation's own name for the Labor ID.
9. **Pre-departure orientation — Art. 78(1):** the Ministry or the appropriate authority provides **pre-deployment and pre-travel awareness-creation training** on conditions in the destination country, the skills needed there, and the worker's rights and responsibilities, as a regular service.

## Who pays for what — Art. 19

**The employer covers (Art. 19(1)):**

a) entry visa fee for the country of destination
b) round-trip transport cost
c) cost of medical examination and vaccination
d) work permit fee
e) residence permit fee
f) insurance coverage
g) visa and document-authentication costs paid to the destination country's embassy or consulate in Ethiopia
h) the employment-contract approval service fee

**The worker covers (Art. 19(2)):**

a) passport issuance fee
b) cost of criminal record clearance
c) birth certificate issuance fee
d) cost of training
e) expense for the certificate of occupational competence

**Art. 19(3)** — for employment secured through personal effort, collaboration and partnership, or as skilled labour, or anything outside 19(1) and 19(2), the service fee is covered as agreed between employer and worker, with the Ministry's approval.

**Art. 19(4)** — if the worker paid the 19(2) costs and is **not deployed through the fault of the employer or agency**, the agency or employer must refund the worker.

**Art. 19(5)** — if the worker, without justified cause, fails to be deployed after all departure requirements are fulfilled, the employer may require the worker to refund the 19(1) expenses.

**Art. 20, service fee** — the Ministry charges the **employer** a service fee for contract approval, agency licence issue and renewal, penalties and similar, under a Council of Ministers regulation.

## What a worker may never be charged

- **Art. 83(1)** — it is **prohibited for any person to undertake overseas employment service by taking payments from a worker.**
- **Art. 50(4)(i)** — "receiving fee, in cash or in kind, from a worker in return for overseas employment service" is a ground for **licence revocation**.
- **Art. 50(4)(v)** — "exacting additional payments other than those recognized by this proclamation as costs imputable to the worker" — also **licence revocation**.
- **Art. 50(4)(u)** — withholding the worker's salary or remittance — **licence revocation**.
- **Art. 50(4)(q) and (s)** — withholding or refusing to hand over the worker's legal travel documents before recruitment or after the contract is approved is revocation; documents in the agency's hands must be returned **within seven consecutive days** of the worker asking.

## Direct employment — prohibited with three exceptions (Art. 8)

No employer may directly recruit and employ a worker except through government or an agency (8(1)). The Ministry may permit direct employment where (8(2)):

a) the employer is a **staff member of an Ethiopian Mission**;
b) the employer is an **international organization**; or
c) the job seeker **found the job by his own effort**, including seafarers.

A skilled or semi-skilled citizen who finds work by personal effort may be permitted after the Ministry checks the 8(6) requirements (8(3)). Vacancies for government-initiated direct employment may be advertised **only** through the Ministry or the relevant authority (8(4)). Direct employment under 8(4) is permitted only when (8(5)): (a) the Ministry confirms through the mission in the destination country, or that country's mission in Ethiopia if there is none abroad, with the employer's legality verified by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, that the worker's rights, safety and dignity can be protected; (b) **life and bodily-injury compensation insurance under Art. 66(1) is in place**; and (c) evidence of convenient transportation is presented with the employment contract.

## Destination-country agreement — the gate on deployment (Art. 21)

Deployment is possible **only**:

a) where there is a **bilateral labour agreement or memorandum of understanding** between the Government of Ethiopia and the government of the receiving country, **or** between the Ministry and the equivalent government authority or an accredited employing company in the receiving country;
b) this does **not** apply to the overseas employment of **seafarers**;
c) notwithstanding (a) and (b), the Ministry may permit an agency that has found employment for **skilled labour** to enter into an employment agreement in line with the agency's classification level.

**Art. 21(2)** — for work secured by **personal effort**, the Ministry may grant permission once it confirms only the preconditions in **Articles 15 to 18** are met.

**Art. 6(5)** — for seafarers, it must be confirmed that the destination country is a member of the international maritime labour convention.

## Labour attaché (Art. 22)

- **22(1)** — the Ministry, in consultation with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, **shall assign a Labor Attaché to the mission in the receiving country** to ensure the rights, safety and dignity of workers employed overseas are protected.
- **22(2)** — for receiving countries with no Ethiopian mission, the Ministry and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs decide and implement how a labour attaché is assigned or how the attaché's duties are carried out.
- **22(3)** — the **number, duties and responsibilities** of labour attachés are set by an operational directive issued jointly by the Ministry and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

## The Ethiopian Overseas Employment Board (Art. 23)

Established by this proclamation. Chair appointed by the **Prime Minister**; the **Ministry of Labor and Skills is Secretary**. Members: Ministry of Foreign Affairs; Ministry of Health; Ministry of Justice; Federal Police Commission; Immigration and Citizenship Service; **National Bank of Ethiopia**; Federation of Agencies; Financial Intelligence Service; National Intelligence and Security Service; **Ethiopian Airlines**; and other relevant institutions as appropriate.

Powers (23(4)): follow up and ensure implementation of the proclamation; facilitate bilateral agreements and MoUs with receiving countries; ensure the rights, dignity and interests of deployed citizens are respected; ensure action is taken when rights are violated or harm occurs abroad; ensure legal action against those involved in illegal overseas employment; facilitate information exchange; ensure public awareness; set directions for institutions promoting legal overseas employment; **may reduce or increase the contributions required of Ethiopians, people of Ethiopian origin and foreign citizens who want to establish an agency under Art. 31(4)**; and issue its own internal structure and operational directives.

## Agency associations, federation, and commission negotiation (Arts. 24–25, 70)

- **Art. 24(1)** — **more than three hundred** agencies may establish an association; associations may form a federation (24(2)). The federation must implement the law, **report to the Ministry every three months**, report agencies and associations involved in illegal activity, notify the Ministry of agreements and commission-negotiation results with destination-country agencies, represent agencies on the Board, and may be allowed to take part in bilateral negotiations (24(3)).
- **Art. 25** — it is **prohibited** to conduct agreements and commission negotiation with destination countries **without the knowledge of the Ministry**; an agency or association that does so has its **licence revoked** and the negotiation outcome is **invalid**.
- **Art. 70** — the Ministry monitors commission negotiation between domestic agencies and agencies in receiving countries, **sets the minimum amount of commission**, and supports as needed. Commission negotiation may be undertaken only by the **federation of agencies** with the Ministry's prior approval. Every agency must submit its commission agreement for each destination country and evidence of commission earned per worker, and the Ministry follows up that commission is **legally remitted to Ethiopia**. Commission may vary by destination country, skill level and profession.

## The worker's contract, and its approval

- **Art. 26 — model contract of employment.** The Ministry prepares a model contract containing **at least**: (a) regular working time, favourable wages for regular hours and overtime pay, **which shall not be lower than the appropriate minimum wage set by the bilateral agreement between Ethiopia and the receiving country, or in the international agreements ratified by both countries**; (b) annual leave, weekly rest and similar; (c) free transport to and from the worksite or an offsetting benefit; (d) free medication, food and accommodation or offsetting benefits for work-related accidents; (e) **life and disability insurance coverage**; (f) a procedure for resolving disputes under the contract; (g) valid reasons for termination. It is prepared **in the working language the worker understands, in English, and in the employer's language** (26(2)), and must take account of fundamental labour laws and policies, the culture, customs, way of life and labour and social laws of the receiving country and the employing enterprise's policies, relevant bilateral and multilateral treaties and conventions, and prevailing labour-market conditions (26(3)).
- **Art. 27 — freedom of the parties.** The parties may stipulate other terms and benefits not in the model contract, provided they do not contravene Ethiopian government policy, law or public morality and are **more beneficial to the worker** than the model contract's minimum.
- **Art. 45 — approval.** Deployment through government-to-government, agency or personal effort must be reduced to a contract following the Ministry's model, **signed by the employer, the worker and, as appropriate, the agency, and approved through the labor market information system or by the Ministry**. The employer or agency submits the contract to the Ministry after the appropriate authority confirms the legal requirements are met (45(2)).
- **Art. 46 — deployment and reporting.** An agency must **deploy the worker within 15 days after the contract is approved**, and must confirm that the deployed worker has obtained a residence permit (or a legal document allowing residence) and a work permit, and **report this to the Ministry within 30 days**. For government-to-government deployments the Ministry follows up the same 30-day permit position.
- **Art. 47 — revocation of an approved contract.** If the agency cannot deploy within the Art. 46 time limit, it must notify the Ministry with reasons and evidence **within 30 consecutive days** asking for the approved contract to be revoked; the Ministry decides **within 5 working days**.
- **Art. 48 — joint and several liability.** Where the contract is breached by the employer, **the agency and the employer are jointly and severally liable** to the worker.
- **Art. 81 — burden of proof.** Where a deployed worker brings a court case about working conditions and the agency or employer objects, **the burden of proof lies on the agency or employer** to show the action has no legally valid ground.

## Licensing an agency

- **Art. 30** — anyone wishing to operate as an agency must obtain a licence from the Ministry. Except for seafarer deployment, the applicant must choose **one of five levels** and meet that level's requirements. A licence is needed **for each country** of operation, and that is stated on the licence. Licences are processed through the Ministry's LMIS.
- **Art. 31 — eligibility and capital.** The applicant must not be barred from the sector. A sole proprietor must be an **Ethiopian national** and meet the capital for the chosen level:

| Level | Capital, shares or contribution |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | **Birr 20,000,000** (twenty million Birr) |
| Level 2 | **Birr 15,000,000** (fifteen million Birr) |
| Level 3 | **Birr 10,000,000** (ten million Birr) |
| Level 4 | **Birr 7,500,000** (seven million five hundred thousand Birr) |
| Level 5 | **Birr 5,000,000** (five million Birr) |

A business organization applicant must be established for overseas employment service, **all members must be Ethiopian citizens**, with paid-up capital as above (31(3)). **Exception for skilled labour (31(4)):** a foreign national of Ethiopian origin, or a foreign national, may jointly establish and operate an agency with an Ethiopian — (a) **75% Ethiopian / 25% foreign national of Ethiopian origin**, or (b) **80% Ethiopian / 20% foreign national** — with paid-up contribution not less than the level's figure. **31(5):** for seafarers and similar sectors needing high sectoral knowledge, international experience and professional networks, the Ministry may allow foreign nationals **full share ownership**. **31(6):** the Art. 66(1) guarantee is deposited **in Birr by Ethiopians and Ethiopian-origin nationals and in foreign currency by foreign nationals, in proportion to shareholding**. **31(7):** a bank or share certificate confirming the initial capital must be submitted.

- **Art. 32 — who may not be licensed.** (1) Any agency, its members, or the board of directors of a share company, whose licence was **revoked, or suspended more than three times**, in breach of 923/2016, 1246/2021, this proclamation or its regulations and directives; or whose licence was revoked for failing to remedy complaints about violations of workers' rights, safety and dignity. (2) Anyone **convicted, or charged and awaiting decision**, for human trafficking, smuggling of migrants, organized crime, terrorism, narcotics trafficking, money laundering, fraud, corruption, laundering property obtained by crime, terrorism financing or illegal arms trafficking. (3) A **travel agency**, airline ticket office, their employees, or transiters. (4) Any official or employee of the Ministry, the appropriate authority, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Immigration and Citizenship, the Police Commission or any government organ implementing this proclamation, or a leader of an Ethiopian community organization abroad — **and the families of those individuals**.
- **Art. 33 — levels.** Licences run from Level One to Level Five based on the number of workers deployed, workers' skill sets, professional mix, startup capital and other criteria.
- **Art. 34 — documents for a licence.** Business registration and trade name certificate; authenticated memorandum of association; **criminal record clearance from the police plus biometrics** for the individual, partners, board and management, supervisors and employed staff; organizational chart, staffing list with educational qualifications and each officer's and employee's job position and personal history; office address and title deed or authenticated lease; **verification that the Art. 66(1) security bond is complied with**; educational credentials of the general manager and staff; confirmation that the **general manager holds at least a first degree and three years of experience in a managerial position**; a pre-licence training certificate for the owner and general manager (or members and management of a business organization); in the destination country — verification of a full-fledged office or a duly licensed representative who is a citizen of that country, verification that the representative accepted the delegation, confirmation of a place providing **temporary food and shelter** for workers, and the **commission fee contract document**; tax clearance for prior licensed operation; and confirmation that the licence fee was paid. All of it is registered and submitted **through the LMIS**.
- **Art. 35 — issuance.** Where the requirements are met the Ministry issues the licence for the level; a refusal is notified to the applicant **in writing within 7 working days**.
- **Art. 36 — validity: one year.**
- **Art. 37** — the licence must be **displayed in a conspicuous place** in the office.
- **Art. 38** — a licence may **never** be transferred, directly or indirectly. No person may be owner, partner, PLC member, board member, general manager or employee of **more than one agency**. Partners or members may not be added or removed without prior notice to the Ministry. On the death of a sole proprietor the licence may devolve to judicially ascertained heirs on request, solely to protect deployed workers and wind the business up; if the heirs want to continue permanently the Ministry may transfer the licence once they meet the requirements. If the heirs do not continue, damage to deployed workers not covered by insurance and the security deposit is **covered from the inheritance**, and monetary liability passes to the heirs in proportion to their share.
- **Arts. 39–40** — any change of board, general manager, or recruitment or dismissal of an employee must be notified to the Ministry **immediately in writing**. An office address may be changed only after the Ministry or appropriate authority verifies the office; a refusal is answered with reasons **within seven working days**; an approved change must be publicised on a legally recognised mass-media outlet and evidenced to the Ministry **within seven working days**.
- **Art. 41 — renewal.** A licence is renewed **every year**. The agency applies **one month before expiry** through the LMIS with: evidence the Art. 66(1) guarantee is on deposit **at the exchange rate of the day**; tax payment evidence; police criminal-record clearance for the Art. 34(3) persons; evidence of the renewed licence of its representative in the receiving country; a general report on deployed and returned workers; and other evidence set by directive. The Ministry renews **within three working days** once satisfied and the renewal fee is paid, or notifies refusal in writing within the same time. An unrenewed licence **remains suspended** (41(4)). If the agency misses the deadline, the licence may be renewed **within one month** after expiry; in the **next one month** it may be renewed on payment of **Birr 100,000.00 (one hundred thousand Birr)**; after that the **licence is revoked** (41(5)). Force majeure proved by evidence and decided by the head of the institution permits renewal (41(6)).
- **Art. 42 — licensed but not deploying.** An agency that deploys **no worker within six months** of getting its licence receives a **written warning**. An agency that fails to deploy **at least 25 workers within 30 working days** of that warning has its **licence revoked immediately**. Any licensed agency that fails to deploy **at least 50 workers within its licence validity period** has its **licence revoked**. None of this applies where the agency could not deploy because **the Ministry did not assign workers** and the agency can produce legal written evidence.
- **Art. 43** — the Ministry grants recognition and incentives based on the Art. 33 levels, the number of destination countries, the skills of deployed workers and similar criteria, under an implementation directive.
- **Art. 79** — the Ministry decides by directive how many Ethiopian agencies a single licensed representative in a destination country may represent, and how many representatives an Ethiopian agency may work with there.

## Inspection (Art. 29)

The Ministry or appropriate authority assigns an **overseas employment inspector** who follows up and inspects whether agencies are meeting their duties. The inspector's powers: verify, **before licensing, before renewal, or before an office address is changed**, that the agency has a fully equipped office and the necessary expertise; ensure the agency has a technology-supported information management system; investigate where the inspector knows or is told that the agency has contravened the proclamation, regulations or directives; monitor to prevent illegal recruitment and deployment; and **enter any agency office at any time during working hours** to examine documents, records, books of accounts and similar, and make inquiries. The inspector must **carry and show the identity card** issued by the Ministry (29(3)), submit findings and recommended legal measures (29(4)), and is **accountable for abuse of power** (29(6)). Every agency has a **duty to cooperate** with the inspector (29(7)).

## Offences and measures against agencies (Arts. 49–52)

**Art. 49** — the Ministry or appropriate authority hears and decides administrative matters arising from breaches of the proclamation and its regulations and directives.

**Art. 50(1) — written warning:** obstructing or trying to obstruct the labour inspector; failing to execute legitimate orders and notices of the Ministry or appropriate authority; failing to display the licence conspicuously.

**Art. 50(2) — first commission entails suspension of the licence for three consecutive months:** appointing a general manager or board member or recruiting an employee without prior notice; **deploying a worker with no occupational competency certificate**; failing without good cause to deploy within the legal time limit; **failing to remedy complaints about violations of workers' rights, safety and dignity**; failing to report deployed workers, vacancies, foreign currency earned as commission, or commission reductions through the established system; failing to report workers whose employment was terminated; **failing to refund the expenses of a worker not deployed through no fault of his own**; failing to verify and report immediately to the Ministry on **bodily injury or death** of a worker abroad with causes and remedial measures; failing to notify an office-address change within the Art. 40 time limit; failing to comply with Art. 66(3); failing to provide **food and temporary shelter to workers within 48 hours of their flight**; keeping other workers in that food-and-shelter facility; or committing an Art. 50(1) offence **more than once within 30 consecutive days**.

**Art. 50(3) — escalation:** a **second** time, or two offences — **six (6) months' suspension**. A **third** time, or three different offences — **twelve (12) months' suspension**.

**Art. 50(4) — licence revocation**, including: the owner, general manager or employee being involved directly or indirectly in a travel agency or airline ticket office (and, for companies, in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Immigration and Citizenship Service or embassies); failing to renew the licence on time; **deploying a worker under 18**; obtaining or renewing a licence on falsified evidence; deploying to a **prohibited destination** or to a country outside the licence; deploying to work that damages public health or morality or tarnishes the country's image; transferring the licence or changing ownership contrary to the proclamation; **taking money in cash or kind from a worker**; deploying **before the Ministry approves the contract**; sending a worker to a destination other than the one in the contract; operating outside the service type or workplace in the licence; transferring a worker to a different employer without revising the contract; involving a person barred from the sector; giving **false information, documents or advertisements** to deploy a worker; substituting or replacing the approved contract without notifying and obtaining the Ministry's approval; **withholding or refusing to hand over the worker's legal travel documents**; **preparing or supplying falsified travel documents**; failing to return the worker's travel documents **within one week** of request; committing a crime against the LMIS; **withholding the worker's salary or remittance**; **exacting payments beyond those the proclamation puts on the worker**; breaching the Art. 25 commission-negotiation restriction; or committing an Art. 50(2) offence **a fourth time**.

**Art. 50(5)–(6)** — these offences also entail **criminal liability** under the Criminal Code and other laws, in addition to the administrative measures.

**Art. 51 — effect of suspension:** the agency must stop all overseas employment service; the Ministry notifies the relevant organs **and the public**; suspension does not release the agency from workers' claims; the Ministry lifts the suspension once the cause is rectified by the deadline and **revokes the licence if it is not**.

**Art. 52 — effect of revocation:** the agency stops all service; the public is notified; revocation does not release it from workers' claims; workers already in process are **transferred to other active agencies** under a Ministry directive.

**Art. 59 — order of execution against an agency:** first against the **agency's blocked bank account holding the workers' security bond**, then against the movable property of the sole proprietor or of the business organization.

## Offences by employers and agency representatives (Arts. 60–63)

**Art. 60(1) — employer:** failing to discharge obligations under the contract; **withholding or denying access to the worker's legal travel documents**; withholding without good cause the worker's wage or remittances; **causing serious injury, health problems or death**; acts affecting the worker's human dignity and morals; **sexual harassment of the worker**; breaching the bilateral agreement or MoU with the destination country; breaching the proclamation, regulations or directives.

**Art. 60(2) — agency's representative abroad:** failing to discharge obligations under the delegation; failing to ensure the employer makes good damage from breach of the worker's rights; failing to provide necessary food, drink and shelter until the worker is deployed; cancelling a work order given to the Ethiopian agency for an unknown reason; breaching the proclamation, regulations or directives.

**Art. 63 — consequences:** the **employer is prohibited from employing workers from Ethiopia**; the **representative is prohibited from participating in Ethiopian overseas employment activity**; the employer or representative **pays adequate compensation** for the damage caused; and the Minister facilitates accountability by the destination-country government under the bilateral agreement or MoU and that country's law.

**Art. 61(3)** — an employer or representative against whom a complaint is lodged **may be suspended** from employing Ethiopian workers or from the sector until the complaint is decided. **Art. 61(4)** — decision **within 15 working days**.

## Offences by a worker (Art. 64)

Grounds: failing to discharge contractual obligations; committing crimes punishable in Ethiopia or the destination country; acts contrary to the religion, custom or traditional practice of the destination country; unwillingness without good cause to be deployed after contract approval; unauthorised use or taking of the employer's or a colleague's money or property; providing false information; terminating the contract without good cause; deserting the place of employment without complying with legal requirements; and similar offences.

Measures for **one** offence (64(2)): first time — **suspended from overseas employment for one year**; second time — **two years**; third time — **disqualified from overseas employment**. For **two or more** offences at once or at different times (64(3)): first time — **two years**; second time — **disqualified**. This is without prejudice to liability under other laws.

## Complaints, decisions and appeal (Arts. 53–58, 61–62, 65, 74–75, 80, 82)

- **Art. 53** — the **victim worker, his legal representative, or a family member** may submit an **oral or written** complaint to the Ministry or appropriate authority alleging that an agency has breached the proclamation, a regulation, a directive or any other relevant law. An oral complaint is written down by the officer and signed by the complainant (53(2)). The Ministry may also act **on its own initiative** or on reports from other bodies (53(3)). "Family" means spouse, parent, descendant, adopted child, or a blood or marriage relative **up to the second degree** (53(4)).
- **Art. 54 — what a complaint must contain:** complainant's name and address; the agency's name and address; the nature, facts and reasons; time and place of the act; the amount of money claimed if any; the relief sought; and other supporting evidence.
- **Art. 55 — hearing:** a file is opened; summons issued with a copy of the complaint and supporting documents, directing the respondent agency to file its defence **within three to seven working days** depending on the case. If it does not answer, the proceeding **resumes ex parte**. Withdrawal by the complainant does **not** bar the Ministry from continuing and deciding. **A decision is rendered within 15 working days.** Criminal elements found in the investigation are reported to the concerned body.
- **Art. 56 — temporary measures:** until the complaint is decided the Ministry **will not approve any contract** submitted by the respondent agency, and **suspends the agency's licence** within 15 working days of filing where the agency fails to appear or produce documents, where continued operation would probably lead to further violation or exploitation or harm relations with a country or the national interest, or where there is prima facie evidence of a breach.
- **Arts. 57–58** — the decision must be precise and short and state the material facts, the issue framed, the evidence, the applicable law, regulation or directive, the reasoning and conclusion, and the decision. An **execution order** is issued, and the respondent agency must **execute it within 15 consecutive days**.
- **Arts. 61–62** — the same route applies to complaints against an **employer or an agency's representative**, lodged by the victim worker **or any person**; decision within **15 working days**.
- **Art. 65 — appeal:** a party aggrieved by the Ministry's or appropriate authority's decision may appeal **within 15 working days** of receiving it to the **Federal High Court or a regional court having jurisdiction**. The court's decision **on issues of fact is final**.
- **Arts. 74–75 — conciliation:** the Ministry or appropriate authority may receive and conciliate complaints against a worker, agency or employer. It notifies the respondent **within three working days** and tries to bring the parties to an amicable settlement **within fifteen working days**. A settlement reached is **binding and enforceable**. If conciliation fails the complainant may take the case to the adjudication office **within five working days**.
- **Art. 80 — labour dispute resolution:** any dispute between the worker and the employer or agency over rights and interests is heard **before a Labour Bench established under the Labour Proclamation** (i.e. Labour Proclamation No. 1156/2019).
- **Art. 82 — limitation:** an administrative complaint about an offence by an employer, agency, representative or worker is barred unless submitted to the Ministry or appropriate authority **within three years** of the offence being committed or becoming known.

## Money guarantees and insurance (Arts. 66–69)

**Art. 66 — the agency's financial guarantee (bond).** Every agency deposits money, in **USD or its equivalent in Birr**, in a bank account opened by the Ministry for this purpose, to guarantee the rights, dignity and safety of deployed workers:

| Agency level | Guarantee deposit |
|---|---|
| Level 1 | **USD 250,000** (two hundred fifty thousand USD) |
| Level 2 | **USD 200,000** (two hundred thousand USD) |
| Level 3 | **USD 150,000** (one hundred fifty thousand USD) |
| Level 4 | **USD 100,000** (one hundred thousand USD) |
| Level 5 | **USD 50,000** (fifty thousand USD) |

Where the agency fails in its duty the Ministry may **withdraw from the deposit** and use it to secure the worker's rights, dignity and safety (66(2)). Once drawn on, the agency must **replenish the fund within 10 working days** (66(3)) — failure to do so is an Art. 50(2) offence. When an agency ceases operation the guarantee is released only when there is **no pending claim** before a competent organ, **six months after the employment contracts of all workers it deployed have terminated**, and after it submits a report showing the status of those workers (66(4)). Details are set by directive (66(5)).

**Art. 67 — Foreign Employers' Guarantee Fund.** Established to secure monetary claims arising from breach of contract for workers employed through the Ministry by a foreign governmental organization or company, and for workers of a foreign employer allowed direct employment. Such an employer must deposit **USD 100 (one hundred USD) for each worker it recruits** into the fund. The Ministry may amend the amount by directive in line with market conditions. The fund **may not be attached or set off** for any claim.

**Art. 68 — Workers' Welfare Fund.** Any worker **may** contribute from his income to the established workers' welfare fund. (Voluntary — the proclamation sets no rate.)

**Art. 69 — insurance.** The **employer must buy life and disability insurance from the domestic insurance market** for every worker deployed overseas. The agency, employer or worker must present the **insurance policy document together with the contract of employment** to the Ministry.

## Support, repatriation and emergencies (Arts. 71–77)

- **Art. 71** — the Ministry and, as appropriate, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs are responsible for ensuring the rights, safety and dignity of deployed workers. An agency must make the employer respect the contract. Training institutions and one-stop centres must discharge their responsibilities.
- **Art. 72** — the Ministry or appropriate authority may receive a request for support from the worker **or from a person who has concern for the worker**, and orders the agency to take remedial action and report on the worker's condition. If the agency or employer does not remedy it, the Ministry **may take administrative measures**. The Ministry also facilitates **financial, training and entrepreneurship support for returnees**, with details in a directive.
- **Art. 73** — workers coming home on leave or vacation and returning to the same employer register the following in the LMIS: valid passport; valid visa and work permit confirmed by the concerned body; a **work identification number** if not already obtained; and other evidence the Ministry requires. Workers whose permits expired but who return to the same employer need a valid passport and valid visa and work permit. Workers renewing or extending an expired contract **without returning to Ethiopia** must submit a valid passport, the contractual document evidencing the renewal or extension, and a valid visa and work permit attested by the relevant authority.
- **Art. 76 — repatriation.** Every agency must: (a) on termination of the contract, **transport the worker and his personal belongings home, covering the air-transport fee**; (b) where the worker suffers **serious bodily injury or dies**, repatriate the injured worker or the body with personal effects, accompanied by a legal medical certificate on the injury or death, **within 15 consecutive days** unless there is valid reason for delay, and cover all associated air-transport expenses; and (c) **cover medical expenses** for a worker repatriated because of serious bodily injury. If the worker terminates the contract **without good cause** the agency may claim back the repatriation expenses (76(2)).
- **Art. 77 — emergency response.** With the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and other concerned ministries and institutions, a system is established for **mass repatriation** in urgent natural or man-made disasters, epidemics and similar emergencies.

## Awareness, prohibition of departure, and directive-making

- **Art. 78** — pre-deployment and pre-travel awareness training for workers; public awareness with the agency federation and through mass media; regular awareness programmes for agency boards, general managers and staff; and briefings for **foreign employers** on Ethiopian recruitment rules, laws, regulations, directives and preconditions.
- **Art. 83(2)** — the Ministry **may prohibit departure** for overseas employment where the destination country is believed to be a **war zone, or affected by natural calamities or contagious diseases**, or similar reasons endangering citizens' safety.
- **Art. 84** — the **Council of Ministers** issues regulations; the **Ministry** issues directives.
- **Art. 85** — everyone has a duty to cooperate in implementing the proclamation.

## Drafting points worth knowing before quoting a cross-reference

Two internal cross-references in the gazette text do not match the enacted numbering, and a reader chasing them will land in the wrong article:

- **Art. 66(2)** refers to "paragraph (a) and (b) of **Article 76(1)**" for the duty the agency failed to execute. Article 76 is *Repatriation of Workers*; the duty and the bond amounts are in Article 66(1)(a)–(e) itself.
- **Art. 45(1)** says the contract shall follow "the model contract prepared by the Ministry pursuant to **Article 27**". The model contract is **Article 26**; Article 27 is *Freedom of Contracting Parties*.

Article 50(2) also lettered its paragraphs ሀ–ኀ in Amharic against a) and then c)–n) in English, so the English sub-paragraph letters in that one list run one behind the Amharic after the second item.
