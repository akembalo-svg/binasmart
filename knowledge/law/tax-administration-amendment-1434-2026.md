# Tax Administration Proclamation (Amendment) No. 1434/2026 — የታክስ አስተዳደር (ማሻሻያ) አዋጅ ቁጥር 1434/2018

Federal Negarit Gazette 32nd Year No. 48, Addis Ababa, 30 July 2026; in force on publication (Art. 3). Amends the Federal Tax Administration Proclamation No. 983/2016. From the Ministry of Revenue's bilingual PDF on mor.gov.et; the English column is reproduced below.

## What this amendment covers (in brief)

Conciliation of tax disputes with the tax authority, led by an independent conciliator; longer time limits for amending assessments where there is tax fraud; limits on new evidence at objection and appeal; a procedure for correcting errors in an assessment; conditional tax clearance; heavier penalties for not issuing invoices; and a renumbering of most of the 983/2016 articles.

## Key provisions

- Renumbering (Art. 2(8), 2(12), 2(20)) — new Articles 61–71 (conciliation) and 97–102 (revising assessments) are inserted, so the old Articles 61–85 of 983/2016 are now 72–96 and the old Articles 86–139 are now 103–156. Tax clearance, formerly Art. 61, is now Art. 72; the notice of appeal, formerly Art. 88, is now Art. 105.
- Art. 2(2) — new definitions: conciliation, conciliator (appointed by the Office of the Prime Minister, with no power to make a binding decision), tax fraud (false books or invoices, concealed income, false returns, parallel accounts, false deductions or refund claims), and new evidence.
- Art. 2(3) — registration follows the International Standard Industrial Classification; the authority may segment taxpayers by directive and must publish a citizens' charter of its service standards.
- Art. 2(4)–(5) — amended assessments: within 10 years of the self-assessment where there is evidence of tax fraud; otherwise within 5 years of the self-assessment or of the assessment notice.
- Art. 2(7) — new Art. 59: the taxpayer carries the burden of proof; evidence not given at the assessment may be brought at objection, appeal or error correction only if ignoring it would cause a heavy tax liability, it was obtained after the assessment, or force majeure prevented it. Art. 2(14): a taxpayer who produces withheld evidence on the first ground pays a penalty of 10% of the tax that would have been due.
- Art. 2(8) — new Arts. 61–71, conciliation: only after an objection and only for an appealable decision; a taxpayer who has appealed may ask for conciliation within 30 days of filing the appeal; not allowed where fraud, intentional evasion or another tax crime is suspected; completed within 60 days; confidential; a signed agreement binds both sides for that taxpayer and period only; if it fails the taxpayer may still appeal; the taxpayer pays the conciliator's fee. Asking for conciliation does not stop the duty to pay the undisputed tax.
- Art. 2(9) — tax clearance (now Art. 72) gains sub-articles 6–11: a conditional clearance for a taxpayer with a pending objection, appeal or dispute, or a valid instalment agreement, usable to renew a business or professional licence, to bid, for the annual inspection of vehicles or construction machinery, and to obtain a bank loan; it does not waive the debt. Banks must see a tax clearance before letting a foreign investor repatriate profits, dividends or sale proceeds.
- Art. 2(10) — the authority will issue a directive on the electronic tax system, invoices with a QR code and e-commerce.
- Art. 2(11)–(12) — new Arts. 96–102: the authority may revise an assessment notice for a computational error, evidence not considered, inconsistency with a binding ruling or another mistake; revision within 5 years of the assessment or 1 year from discovering the error, whichever ends first; only once for the same error.
- Art. 2(13) — new Art. 105(1): an appeal to the Tax Appeal Commission is filed in the approved form within 30 consecutive days of service of the decision.
- Art. 2(15) — Art. 123(2): where a body or public office fails to withhold, its manager, head, chief accountant or responsible employee each pay Birr 2,000, if the tax was not withheld and paid before the authority asked.
- Art. 2(16) — new Art. 125: failure to issue a tax invoice, Birr 100,000 for each invoice not issued; the penalty may not be waived.
- Art. 2(17) — Art. 137: understating the price on invoices, fine of Birr 100,000 and five to seven years' rigorous imprisonment; criminal liability for not issuing an invoice only after two administrative penalties for the same act within one tax period.
- Art. 2(18) — new Art. 149: for an offence by a body or sole proprietorship, the manager or financial head is deemed to have committed it unless they prove they did not know, did not permit it, or had an internal control system able to prevent it.
- Art. 2(19) and 2(21) — a taxpayer with an appeal pending when this law came into force may apply for conciliation within 60 days; pending cases are decided under the amended proclamation.

983/2016 WHEREAS, it has become necessary to foster the country's economic growth by ensuring a balanced enforcement of the rights and obligations of taxpayers within the tax administration system, thereby strengthening operational efficiency, equity, and compliance with tax obligations; WHEREAS, modern tax systems increasingly utilize conciliation and other alternative dispute resolution mechanisms; WHEREAS, it has become necessary to harmonize and render the tax dispute resolution system in Ethiopia consistent with international best practices and principles, with a view to enhancing its acceptability and efficacy; WHEREAS, it has become necessary to adapt the tax administration legal frameworks to align with and take into account the dynamic and rapidly growing domestic economic activities; NOW, THEREFORE, in accordance with

1/ Office of the Prime Minister shall appoint an independent and impartial Conciliator.
2/ The Conciliator appointed in accordance with Sub Article (1) of this Article shall be impartial and qualified.

1/ The conciliator shall act as an independent and impartial professional to assist the disputing parties in reaching a fair resolution to the dispute.

b) for purposes of facilitating amicable settlement to assist the tax Authority and the taxpayer in thoroughly examining the factual; legal; technical and evidentiary issues underlying the dispute;
c) To draft and present proposal containing terms of settlement at any stage of the conciliation process for the consideration of the parties;
d) To render a non-binding professional opinion regarding the potential outcome should the matter proceed to an appellate body or a court of law, in the event that the disputing parties fail to reach an agreement.

1/ the Conciliation process shall be conducted in the following manner:
a) shall be conducted based on neutrality and confidentiality;
b) The Conciliator’s role shall be to facilitate the process but does not decide;
c) The conciliation process may not apply formal procedures .
2/ Conciliation shall be completed within 60 days from the date the parties have agreed to settle the tax dispute through Conciliation.
3/ Notwithstanding the provisions of Sub-Article (2) of this Article, the conditions under which the conciliation process may be extended beyond 60 days shall be determined by a Directive to be issued by the Ministry.
68. Confidentiality
1/ All communications during the process of conciliation shall be kept confidential.
2/ The Conciliation result shall not serve as evidence in any appeal process except as provided by law or the consent of the Parties.
69. Conciliation Agreement
1/ If the parties agree on the conciliation result the agreement shall be:
a) made in writing and signed by both parties;
b) binding and enforceable on both parties.

5/ Where an agreement under this Article is reached between the tax Authority and the taxpayer only on partial matters, the agreement shall be applicable solely to the matters agreed upon.
70. Failure of Conciliation Where the participants of the conciliation fail to agree on the outcome of the conciliation, the taxpayer may file an appeal to the Tax Appeal Commission or to the competent court of law pursuant to
Article 105 of the Proclamation.
71. Costs
1/ Each participant of the conciliation shall bear their own costs incurred for the conciliation process.

96. Power to Revise Assessment

97. Grounds for Amending a Tax Assessment Notice An amendment may be made to a tax assessment notice pursuant to Article 96 of this Proclamation only where:
a) there is a computational or arithmetical error;
b) There is relevant evidence that was not considered during the tax assessment or new evidence presented in accordance with this Proclamation;
