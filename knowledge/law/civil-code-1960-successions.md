# Civil Code of Ethiopia 1960 — Successions (Arts. 826–1125) — የፍትሐ ብሔር ሕግ፦ ውርስ

Civil Code Proclamation No. 165 of 1960, Negarit Gazeta Extraordinary Issue No. 2 of 1960, in force 11 September 1960. Successions are Book II ("Family and Successions"), Title V, Articles 826–1125 — not Book III, which is "Goods". English text from the official 1960 English edition (scan with OCR, so some letters are misread, e.g. "he" for "be"); the Amharic edition available online is a scan without usable text. The family chapters of Book II were replaced by the Revised Family Code 213/2000; the succession title still applies.

## What this part covers (in brief)

What happens to a person's property when they die: who inherits when there is no will, the forms a valid will must take, disinheritance, the certificate of heir, claims by the true heir, and how the estate is liquidated and divided.

## Key articles

- Art. 826 — the succession opens at the deceased's principal residence; rights and obligations pass to heirs and legatees. Art. 829 — succession is intestate (no will), testate (by will), or partly both.
- Art. 838 — an heir who was sentenced for intentionally killing or attempting to kill the deceased (or the deceased's spouse, descendant or ascendant) is unworthy and cannot inherit.
- Art. 842 — first in line are the children, in equal shares; a child who died before is represented by his or her own descendants.
- Arts. 843–844 — if there are no descendants, the father and mother take half each, represented by their descendants if dead.
- Arts. 845–846 — then the grandparents (half to the paternal line, half to the maternal line) or their descendants. Art. 847 — then the great-grandparents. Art. 852 — with no relatives, the estate goes to the State.
- Art. 837 — sex, age and nationality of an heir do not affect inheritance rights. Art. 836 — legitimate, illegitimate and adopted children are treated alike.
- Art. 880 — three kinds of wills: public, holograph (handwritten) and oral. Art. 881 — a public will is read before the testator and four witnesses and signed at once; Art. 882 — two witnesses are enough if one is a registrar or notary acting in that capacity. Art. 884 — a holograph will must be wholly handwritten, say it is a will, and every page must be dated and signed.
- Arts. 937–938 — a will may expressly disinherit an heir; disinheriting a child or other descendant has no effect unless the will gives a reason the court finds justifying.
- Art. 996 — an heir may apply to the court for a certificate of heir stating the share he is called to take; Art. 997 — while not annulled the certificate proves that status; Art. 998 — it can be annulled.
- Arts. 999–1000 — "petitio haereditatis": the true heir may sue whoever took the estate; the claim is barred three years after the heir learned of his right and of the taking, and absolutely after fifteen years.
- Art. 1060 — until partition, the estate is held in common by the heirs.

Note: the surviving spouse is not listed among the heirs-at-law in Arts. 842–852; a spouse's claim to common marital property is governed by the family code (liquidation of common property), and a spouse may also receive property by will.

Article 826. Opening of succession

(1) Where a person dies, the succession of such person, called the d& ceased, shall open at the place where he had his principal residence at the time of his death.

(2) The rights and obligations of the deceased which form the inheritance shall pass to his heirs and legatees, in accordance with the provisions of this Title, unless such rights and obligations terminate by the death of the deceased. 

### Article 827. Things making up inheritance. - l. Life insurance

(1) Monies due in performance of a contract of life insurance to which the deceased was a party, shall form part of the inheritance where the deceased has not detennined the beneficiary or the insurance is made to the benefit of the heirs of the deceased without any other indication. •

(2) In other cases, they shall not form part of the inheritance.

### Article 828. 2. Pensions and itUlemnities

Pensions or indenmities payable to the relatives or to the spouse of the deceased as a consequence of his death shall not form part of the in· heritance.

### Article 829. Different kinds of successions

(1) The succession of the deceased may be either intestate or testate.

(2) It may be partly intestate and partly testate.

(3) The property of which the deceased has not disposed by will shall devolve upon his heirs-at-law.

### Article 830. Conditions required for succeeding

A person may not succeed the deceased unless he survives him and he is not unworthy of succeeding him.

Where two or more persons are dead and it is not possible to proYe which of such persons survived the other, the succession of each one of such persons shall be regulated as if he had been the last survivor without, however, receiving anything from the succession of the other persons.

### Article 833. Death of heir

Where a person who is called to a succession dies after such succession has opened, his rights relating to the succession shall pass to his heirs.
