## What this regulation covers (in brief)

Details of the income tax law: permanent establishments, employment income and the valuation of fringe benefits (debt waiver, household personnel, housing, loans, meals, vehicles, private expenditure, employee shares), rental income, deductible business expenses, depreciation, losses, insurance and banking income, exempt income, books of account for category "B" and "C" taxpayers, withholding agents, and transitional rules.

## Key articles

- Art. 8–19 — the benefits an employer gives an employee that count as fringe benefits, and how each is valued; small benefits that are impractical to account for, and exempt income under Schedule "E", are not fringe benefits.
- Art. 27–35 — deductions: representation expenditure, interest to a foreign lender, employees' medical expenses, business promotion, charitable donations, head office expenses.
- Art. 36–41 — depreciation of business assets (straight-line and diminishing value), repairs and improvements.
- Art. 42–43 — carrying business losses forward and backward.
- Art. 54 — exempt income includes employment income, for up to five years, of expatriate professionals recruited by exporters for knowledge transfer under a directive, and the pay of an unskilled casual employee working for the same employer for no more than 30 days in twelve months; an employer's medical insurance premiums for employees are covered by the medical-cost exemption.
- Art. 58–60 — the Authority sets by directive the documents category "B" taxpayers file with their simplified books; category "C" taxpayers may keep books, and otherwise pay presumptive tax; a category "C" taxpayer who employs workers keeps records of wages and tax withheld.
- Art. 61 — the Minister specifies by directive the services and persons to which withholding does not apply.
- Art. 62–64 — a withholding agent issues a serially numbered official receipt to the payee (authenticated by the Ministry when the agent is a government body); a payee gives the withholding agent a trade licence as well as a TIN; a withholding agent who did not withhold escapes liability for the tax if it shows the payee paid it, but not the penalty.
- Art. 65 — books of account are not rejected merely because they were submitted late, unless tax was already estimated for non-filing.
- Art. 66 — the Authority issues a directive on paying tax on income derived after a business has ceased.
