# MoLS Overseas Employment Implementation Directive — 2018 E.C. draft

**Amharic title:** የኢትዮጵያ የውጭ አገር ሥራ ስምሪት አፈጻጸምን ለመደንገግ የወጣ መመሪያ
**Issuing body:** Ministry of Labor and Skills, Addis Ababa, **2018 ዓ.ም.** (2025/2026 G.C.)
**Legal basis stated in the preamble:** issued by the Ministry of Labour and Skills under **Article 84(2) of Ethiopia's Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 1389/2017 (= 1389/2025)**.
**Structure:** six chapters, 39 articles (with two articles both numbered 35).

## Read this first — it is a draft, and it says so

Three things on the face of the document say it had not been issued when this copy was made:

1. The cover and Article 1 give the citation as **"መመሪያ ቁጥር --/2018"** and **"መመሪያ ቁጥር ----/2018"** — the directive number is a row of dashes.
2. **Article 39 (Effective Date)** reads: *"ይህ መመሪያ ከ______ ጀምሮ ተፈጻሚ ይሆናል"* — "This Directive shall be effective from ______." The date is blank.
3. The closing block names **ሙፈሪሀት ካሚል (Muferihat Kamil), Minister of Labour and Skills**, with no signature and no date.

The PDF was created in Microsoft Word on **16 October 2025**, two months after Proclamation 1389/2025 came into force. So this is the Ministry's implementation directive **as drafted**, and nothing in it should be quoted as being in force. Where it differs from the proclamation, **Proclamation 1389/2025 governs**; Article 87 of that proclamation keeps the directives made under the repealed 923/2016 alive until they are actually replaced.

**Article 38 (Repeal)** states that **Implementation Directive No. 90/2012 E.C.**, issued to implement Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 923/2008 E.C. (= 923/2016), **is repealed by this directive** — which will only take effect when the directive itself does.

## Definitions worth having (Art. 2)

- **"Proclamation"** means Ethiopia's Overseas Employment Proclamation **No. 1389/2017** (= 1389/2025).
- **"Minimum wage"** means the wage floor determined by the bilateral overseas-employment agreement or MoU Ethiopia concludes with receiving countries, or by the employment contract, **based on periodic market study**.
- Definitions in the proclamation **and in Article 2 of Labour Proclamation No. 1156/2011 E.C. (= 1156/2019)** apply as relevant.

## Wages (Art. 4)

- The minimum wage for citizens deployed abroad is determined by the **bilateral agreement or MoU with the receiving country, or by the employment contract**.
- The wage set in the contract **may not be lower** than the amount set in the bilateral agreement or MoU.
- For **skilled labour** deployed through personal effort or an agency, the wage must be **comparable to the pay of the same professional grade in the receiving country, or not lower than that country's minimum wage for the same grade**.

## Medical examination (Art. 5)

- Health institutions carry out **physical and mental examination** and send the result to the Ministry.
- The Ministry and the Ministry of Health jointly verify that selected health institutions are working to the criteria they were selected on.
- **A female worker must, in addition to the initial general examination, take a pregnancy test within the 48 hours before travel and be confirmed not pregnant; the agency must produce the confirmation.**
- If a first examination result is not as required and has to be repeated, **the worker bears the cost of the repeat**, and the Ministry confirms that the first result was rejected.
- The **price** of the medical examination is set jointly by the Ministry and the Ministry of Health on the basis of study.

## Bilateral agreement or MoU (Art. 6)

The Ministry prepares a model bilateral agreement or MoU as the baseline for agreements between an agency and a receiving-country employer for **skilled labour**. The Ministry may permit such an agreement only where: the agency is licensed to send that skilled profession; the employing company's legality (or its recruitment licence) is verified; **the draft agreement is no worse than the Ministry's model and is verified at the Ethiopian mission in the receiving country**; the commission agreement is verified by the destination country's foreign ministry, the labour attaché, or the Ethiopian mission; for seafarers, the agreement is verified by the **Ethiopian Maritime Authority** against the maritime convention; the employing company holds a valid renewed licence in its sector with a good performance and conduct record; and the destination country's diplomatic relations and general situation allow citizens' rights, safety and dignity to be protected. For **seafarers**, an agency may only contract with an agency or employer in a country that has **signed the Maritime Labour Convention 2006 (as amended)** or has an equivalent legal framework.

## Deployment through personal effort (Art. 7)

The Ministry may permit it only where the worker is **skilled labour**; the employer's legality is verified by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the Ethiopian mission in that country; the contract between employer and worker is verified through Foreign Affairs; and convenient transport is confirmed.

The individual must submit: the **employment contract**; the **work and residence visa**; a **renewed kebele ID and the unique worker identification number**; an occupational competency certificate if the employer requires one; an authenticated educational credential; the **travel medical examination**; a **criminal-record clearance certificate**; evidence of **life and bodily-injury compensation insurance under Article 69(1) of the proclamation**; and evidence of payment of the service fee and the employers' guarantee fund.

The Ministry decides **within three working days**, and where it refuses it notifies the reason **within three working days**.

## Cost coverage (Art. 8)

Costs follow **Article 19(1) and (2) of the proclamation**. For workers deployed through personal effort, through collaboration and partnership training, or as skilled labour, costs follow **Article 19(3)** — by agreement between the worker and the employer.

## Labour attaché (Arts. 9–10)

**Duties (Art. 9(1)):** monitor workers' conditions at work; mediate, investigate and resolve conflicts, complaints and disagreements; investigate and resolve or mediate disputes involving workers, receiving-country agencies, employers and government; advise and support citizens on problems in the destination country; act on **insurance claims, medical care, and death and disability compensation claims**; and arrange the return home of citizens who cannot continue working.

**Overseas employment services the attaché delivers (Art. 9(2)):** examine documents and **licence and renew licences** for recruitment agencies and employers in the destination country; **approve job orders**; examine and **approve or renew contracts** for skilled labour against Ethiopian and destination-country law; carry out licensing, renewal, surprise, tip-off-based and routine inspection of agencies, employers and workplaces there; lead and coordinate overseas-employment support offices in countries with no embassy or consulate when instructed; study labour demand and approve agreements; **report to the Ministry monthly** on illegal employment and recruitment, real conditions, opportunities and difficulties; and follow up implementation of bilateral agreements and maintain working relations with government bodies, trade associations, agency associations and diaspora associations.

**Term and accountability (Art. 9(4)–(7)):** an attaché serves **4 years**, extendable by the Ministry; the Ministry may transfer, recall or reassign an attaché; the Ministry provides capacity-building training; and an attaché found engaged in **human trafficking, corruption or malpractice, or repeatedly failing to report**, is held accountable.

**Assignment criteria (Art. 10):** **one labour attaché for up to twenty-five thousand (25,000) citizens deployed in the destination country**, with additional staff as the Ministry finds necessary; plus citizens' vulnerability and the frequency of rights, safety, dignity and welfare complaints; the size of the destination country; and whether a bilateral agreement or MoU has been signed with it.

## One-stop service and the 18-year rule (Art. 11)

Anyone wanting to be deployed abroad must **register on the labour market information system at the nearest one-stop service centre**. To register, the citizen must:

- **be 18 years of age or older;**
- be competent for the work they will do;
- be willing to take the necessary training and assessment at the nearest institution;
- and, where the nearest institution cannot deliver the field they want, be able to take the training at a training centre of their choosing.

The Ministry identifies those who meet the criteria from the LMIS register and sends them to training centres, and may send citizens to training in the place they choose.

## Training and competency assessment (Arts. 12–14)

- Training institutions may be public or private; private ones need the proper training licence, must meet the Ministry's curriculum and related criteria as verified by the appropriate authority, and are then **registered through the LMIS**.
- **Training starts on days 1 to 5 of each month**; trainees assigned but not started may begin in days 1–5 of the following month, and the institution may adjust the training period to the number of trainees. A trainee who missed the window can find out the next start date through the one-stop centre where they registered.
- The institution must issue a **certificate of completion listing the training types taken and the results**, and must **re-train a failed trainee for half the fee already paid**.
- Completed trainees are notified to the LMIS **within three days**, and the LMIS notifies the assessment centre.
- The Ministry rewards good performers, warns institutions found not training properly, and may **bar repeat offenders from overseas-employment training**.
- The **assessment centre** must set an assessment programme and assess trainees **within seven days** of receiving them, report results through the system, certify those who pass, and allow a failed candidate to re-sit at the same centre. Experience-based candidates are assessed against the field they will be deployed in.

## Placement of workers (Art. 15)

An agency's placement request must state the skill type the job needs, the professional sector, sub-sector and profession, the educational level as applicable, and any other employer requirements.

The Ministry places workers **within three working days** after verifying that: the agency is **not under suspension or revocation**; the agency holds a job order for the profession requested; the agency is **licensed for the country the job order came from**; and the agency's **level permits** the request.

- The agency must **deploy the placed worker within 30 working days** (15(4)).
- The agency prepares the worker's travel documents and submits them to the Ministry for approval (15(5)).
- If the agency cannot deploy for reasons outside its control or because of the worker, it must notify the Ministry **within three working days** of learning of it (15(6)), and the Ministry decides after verifying (15(7)).
- **If the Ministry fails to place within 7 working days (15(8)):** for **skilled labour**, the head of the Ministry may permit the agency to recruit by itself; for **semi-skilled or experience-based** workers, the Ministry advertises and recruits.

**Art. 16:** an agency must submit its recruitment performance report on the Ministry's format **within 5 working days** of completing recruitment.

## Agency levels — the operating envelope for each licence class (Art. 17)

The proclamation sets the capital and bond per level; this draft directive adds the office, staffing, structure and market limits:

| | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 | Level 4 | Level 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Professions it may deploy | all professions, including workers with high sectoral knowledge | domestic work, labour-intensive professions, semi-skilled and skilled labour | domestic work and labour-intensive professions | domestic work and labour-intensive professions | **domestic work only** |
| Office floor area | **700 m²** | **500 m²** | **350 m²** | **200 m²** | **100 m²** |
| Accommodation capacity at one time | not less than **100 people** | not less than **80** | not less than **40** | not less than **30** | not less than **10** |
| Staff | **60 and above** | **more than 40** | **more than 20** | **more than 10** | **more than 5** |
| Capital (per proclamation Art. 31) | **Birr 20,000,000** | **Birr 15,000,000** | **Birr 10,000,000** | **Birr 7,500,000** | **Birr 5,000,000** |
| Guarantee (per proclamation Art. 66) | **USD 250,000** | **USD 200,000** | **USD 150,000** | **USD 100,000** | **USD 50,000** |
| Work links (partners) | **not more than 20** per destination country | **not more than 20** | **not more than 16** | **not more than 8** | **not more than 4** |
| Destination countries | **no limit** | **not more than 8** | **not more than 8** | **not more than 4** | **not more than 2** |

Required departments: **Levels 1 and 2** — legal and complaints-hearing; citizen support and follow-up; advice and counselling; IT and digital systems; recruitment and external relations; training and occupational competency team; monitoring and control team. **Levels 3, 4 and 5** — citizen complaints, support and follow-up team; advice and counselling team; IT and digital systems team.

## Extra licence conditions, and the general manager (Arts. 18–19)

**Art. 18**, on top of proclamation Articles 30 and 31: the representative named in the receiving country must produce a **good-conduct statement** from the relevant body there, verified by the Ethiopian mission and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; for skilled labour there must be an employment agreement with a government-recognised employing company; the **guarantee deposited in the blocked account must be in cash and equivalent to the required USD amount at the current exchange rate**; and every agency must **prepare a worker holding area and a sanitation area**.

**Art. 19 — the general manager**, by level (the proclamation's floor is a first degree plus three years in management):

| Level | Qualification | Experience |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | second degree | **7 years** in a similar post, or at least **4 years** in the sector |
| 2 | second degree | **5 years**, or at least **3 years** in the sector |
| 3 | second degree | **3 years**, or at least **2 years** in the sector |
| 4 | first degree | **7 years**, or at least **2 years** in the sector |
| 5 | first degree | **4 years**, or **2 years** in the sector |

## Duty to notify (Art. 20)

- Where a deployed worker suffers **serious bodily injury or death, returns home, goes missing, is arrested, changes employer** or similar, the agency must notify the Ministry with sufficient evidence through the LMIS **within at least 10 working days** of the date and hour of the event, according to its gravity. The Ministry decides **within 7 working days**.
- The agency must submit a **general report on deployed workers every quarter, within 15 days of the start of the month**.
- Where the overseas agency is a company that employs the workers itself, the Ethiopian agency must report **quarterly, within 15 days of the start of the month**, that wages were paid.
- For a claim that a deployed worker is **missing**, police evidence from that country, authenticated by the Ethiopian mission or consulate, must reach the Ministry **within 10 days**.

## Commission (Art. 21)

- Each agency reports the foreign-currency commission per destination country (in **USD**), against the named list of workers, with its agreement with the foreign partner attached, **through the LMIS every 3 months**.
- Commission may be paid **only into the agency's bank account registered on its licence**.
- The commission rate is set by agreement between the sending and receiving countries' agency federations, and **no agency may agree to less than the rate the associations set** — including agencies that are not members.
- Evidence that the foreign currency earned was deposited in a domestic bank must be reported **in the last 3 working days of each quarter**.
- An agency may claim a commission reduction for a returned worker only with evidence and once the Ministry verifies the worker's return through the relevant government body.
- **Where skilled labour (not seafarers) is deployed through an agency, any payment the worker makes to the agency must not exceed one month of the worker's salary**, and only where the employer's payment does not cover the agency's due, verified by the Ethiopian mission or consulate or the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; there must be an employment agreement with a government-recognised employer; and the commission agreement must be approved by the Ministry.

## Licence renewal, branches and the office (Arts. 22–24)

**Art. 22**, on top of proclamation Article 41(2): a support letter from the region where a branch operates; an organizational chart showing current staff and their posts **with photographs**; title deed or an authenticated renewed lease; evidence that the blocked guarantee still matches the foreign-currency requirement; and **evidence that the agency has deployed at least 10 workers a month since it was certified**. Seafarer licensing and renewal is reviewed and approved jointly with the **Ethiopian Maritime Authority**. Licensing and renewal are **online**. A worker holding area and sanitation area must be verified.

**Art. 23** — to open an additional office inside Ethiopia the agency applies to the appropriate authority naming the region, zone and woreda; the authority may permit it after assessing the agency's record on protecting deployed citizens' rights and safety; the authority notifies the Ministry **in writing within 7 days**; and the agency must publicise the branch on a widely circulated newspaper or radio and television **within 7 working days** and produce the evidence.

**Art. 24** — before a licence is issued or renewed or the office address changed, the office must be **easily accessible, clear, and not more than 100 metres from a main or feeder road**, with a room size matching the level, seating for clients, necessary office equipment and a computer for data. Regional branches need staffing and office space matching the level, seats, equipment and a computer.

## The contract of employment (Arts. 25–28)

- **Art. 25** — contracts are built on the **model contract**, and may vary by receiving country and profession. Government-to-government, agency and personal-effort deployments all use it. Once approved, the agency has the worker sign and **prints the contract in 4 copies**: one for the worker, one for the agency, one for the employer and one held in the system database.
- **Art. 26 — documents that must be uploaded to approve a contract:** the contract signed by employer and worker (and the agency as applicable); the **employment visa written at least partly in English**, with the original; a valid copy of the worker's **passport**; a valid copy of the worker's **kebele ID**; a valid copy of the **kebele ID of the worker's next of kin**; a **police fingerprint result certificate** as needed; the **medical examination result** from a health institution selected by the Ministry of Health; a copy of the **occupational competency certificate**; the worker's personal history, **biometric fingerprint and unique worker number**; evidence of **life and bodily-injury insurance**; and, where the employer is a government body or a direct-employment-approved foreign employer, evidence that the **guarantee fund deposit** was paid into the bank.
- **Art. 27** — once the documents are complete and uploaded in the agency's name, the Ministry examines, approves and registers the contract; the same applies to an authority delegated to approve contracts. **An extended or amended contract must again be submitted, approved and registered.** Agencies must upload approval documents online; the Ministry prepares the online-approval manual and a code of conduct and delivers training.
- **Art. 28** — the contract may be terminated under the applicable law and where a ground stated in the contract for termination occurs.

## Insurance, complaints, and the agent ratio (Arts. 30–32)

- **Art. 30** — the Ministry **selects insurers by competition** and notifies the relevant body; the employer arranges life and bodily-injury cover for the worker with a selected insurer; the **cover runs for the duration of the employment contract**; and better cover than this for skilled labour is acceptable.
- **Art. 31** — complaints against an agency, and complaints arising from direct employment and government-to-government deployment, are **submitted to and heard by the Ministry**, which collects the necessary evidence from the relevant bodies.
- **Art. 32** — a licensed representative in a receiving country may act for **only two** Ethiopian agencies (seafarer deployment excepted); an Ethiopian agency may name **only two** representatives in one receiving country (seafarer deployment excepted); and a representative may only switch to another agency **after the contracts of the workers already deployed through the first agency have ended**.

## Returning a licence, releasing the guarantee, and paying out of it (Arts. 33–35)

**Art. 33 — release of the guarantee.** An agency returning its licence must submit: a written application with the licence; a detailed report on the Ministry's format showing the status of every citizen deployed since the licence was issued (or a letter saying none were deployed); a **tax clearance** from the revenue and customs authority; and, where it operated an office, evidence from the region that it is **free of complaints**. The Ministry then: verifies **within 1 month** with the justice bodies, the missions in receiving countries and other relevant bodies that no case or complaint about rights violations is outstanding; publishes a **two-month public notice in the Addis Zemen newspaper** to see whether any claim is raised; verifies that no deployed citizen's contract is still running; and releases the money **within not more than four months** of the request. Agencies licensed under the current or the repealed law that **never deployed a worker** get their guarantee released after that is verified from the database.

**Art. 34 — what the guarantee may be claimed for.** Under proclamation Article 66, a claim against the agency's guarantee may be made to the Ministry **only** where: (1) the worker suffered a **work-related injury in the destination country and needs medical costs covered**; or (2) the worker **needs transport costs to return home**.

**Art. 35 (first of two articles so numbered) — foreign employers' guarantee fund.** Under proclamation Article 67, to claim from the fund for breach of contract: the worker or legal heir must state **in writing** that the wage was not paid under the contract; the database must confirm the worker travelled on a legally approved contract; the unpaid and correct wage claim must be **verified by the labour attaché or the mission in that country and by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs**; and payment is then made under the applicable finance law.

**Art. 35 (second) — incentives.** Better-performing agencies are rewarded under the agency performance-assessment criteria to be issued; and a system is established to reward **citizens by the amount of foreign currency they remitted home legally through banks** while working abroad.

## Cross-reference problems in this draft

The draft's citations to Proclamation 1389/2025 were written against an **earlier article numbering** and do not match the enacted text. Anyone quoting them will land in the wrong article:

| Draft says | Enacted 1389/2025 actually has |
|---|---|
| Art. 6(2): "proclamation Art. 12(3)" for agency-to-employer agreements for skilled labour | Art. 21(1)(c) |
| Art. 26(12): "proclamation Art. 61(2)" for the guarantee fund deposit | Art. 67(2) |
| Arts. 27(1) and 27(2): "proclamation Art. 37" for contract approval | Art. 45 |
| Art. 30: "proclamation Art. 62" for insurance | Art. 69 |
| Art. 35: "proclamation Art. 34" for incentives | Art. 43 |
| Art. 24: "proclamation Art. 35" for verifying the office before licensing | Art. 29(2)(a) |

Only Article 7(2) cites correctly — "proclamation Article 69(1)" for life and bodily-injury insurance, which is right.

Internal numbering faults in the draft: **two articles are numbered 35**; **Article 31 jumps from sub-article 1 to sub-article 3**; **Articles 27(1)–(2) and 33(4) cite "this directive Article 20" and "Article 27"** where they mean the directive's own Articles 26 and 33.

## Disclaimer

This is a working summary of a draft government directive, prepared for reference. It is not an official translation, not legal advice, and — most importantly — **not a statement of law in force**. The directive it summarises has no number and no effective date. For the rules that are binding today, use Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 1389/2025 and the directives made under Proclamation 923/2016 that Article 87 of 1389/2025 keeps alive until they are replaced. For a specific case consult the Ministry of Labor and Skills or a licensed Ethiopian lawyer.
