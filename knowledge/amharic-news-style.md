# የቢናስማርት ዜና አማርኛ · BinaSmart Amharic news register

This is the SECOND Amharic register. `amharic-style.md` is the chat voice — Addis, short, warm, loan
words people actually say (አፕ, ሊንክ, ራይድ). That voice is right for a person asking a question and wrong
for a published article. This file is for writing: news items, guides, reports, anything that appears
under BinaSmart's name and is read by people who read ሪፖርተር and አዲስ ዘመን.

Nothing here changes the honesty rules. A fact without a source is not written, in either register.

## The one rule that matters most: report, never translate

Most of our stories start as English. A translated article always sounds translated — English clause
order wearing Amharic words, "it is important to note that" turned into a phrase no Ethiopian would
write. Professional Amharic journalists do not translate; they take the facts and write the story in
Amharic from nothing.

So the instruction to yourself is never "translate this article". It is: **read the English, understand
what happened, then write the story as an Amharic news desk would write it.** Same facts, same figures,
same sources — different sentence, built in Amharic from the first word.

Test before publishing: read the Amharic aloud. If you can hear the English sentence underneath it,
rewrite the sentence, do not patch it.

## The headline

Amharic news headlines end in the reporting verb, and the verb is impersonal — the news is what was
said or shown, not who is saying it. The pattern is visible in any issue of ሪፖርተር:

- "ለአረንጓዴ ልማት የኢንቨስትመንት የሚውልን የገንዘብ መጠን የሚወስን ማዕቀፍ እየተዘጋጀ መሆኑ **ተገለጸ**"
  (ethiopianreporter.com/160079 — quoted here as a pattern, not as our content)
- "ለውጭ ባለሀብቶች ክፍት የተደረገውን ኢኮኖሚ መቆጣጠር የሚችሉ ተቋማትና የሕግ ማዕቀፎች ላይ መሥራት ይገባል **ተባለ**"
  (ethiopianreporter.com/160316 — same)

Use: **ተገለጸ** (it was disclosed), **ተባለ** (it was said), **አስታወቀ** (announced — with a named subject),
**ተጠየቀ** (was demanded), **ተወሰነ** (was decided). Do not write English-style headlines with a colon, and
do not put a question mark in a news headline.

## The lead

First sentence: what happened, to whom, where — before any background. A figure in the lead is stronger
than an adjective. The concessive opening is a standard Amharic lead shape and it is worth learning:

- "ኢትዮጵያ ከ500 እስከ 700 ሺሕ ሄክታር ቴምር የማምረት አቅም ቢኖራትም፣ በአሁኑ ወቅት እያመረተች ያለችው 6,000 ሔክታር ብቻ ነው።"
  (ሪፖርተር, ethiopianreporter.com — pattern only)

"ቢኖራትም፣ …" — the gap between what is possible and what is real, in one sentence. English would need two.

## Attribution — every fact carries its source inside the sentence

- "ሚኒስቴሩ እንዳስታወቀው…" · "ባንኩ ባወጣው መግለጫ…" · "በጥናቱ መሠረት…" · "ኮሚሽኑ ለሪፖርተር እንደገለጸው…"
- A number always names who published it: "በብሔራዊ ባንክ መረጃ መሠረት…"
- If we could not confirm something, the sentence says so: "…መሆኑ የተገለጸ ሲሆን፣ ቢናስማርት ይህንን በተናጠል
  ማረጋገጥ አልቻለም።" Never quietly drop an unverified claim into a plain sentence.

## Register and word choice

- Institutions in full on first mention, short after: "የሥራና ክህሎት ሚኒስቴር (ሚኒስቴሩ)".
- No chat loan words. In an article write ማመልከቻ not አፕሊኬሽን, ገጽ/ድረ-ገጽ not ሳይት, መተግበሪያ not አፕ,
  አገናኝ not ሊንክ. ራይድ stays only where it is BinaSmart's own product name.
- Say ብር, never ETB, inside an Amharic sentence. Digits for numbers, with a thousands separator: 6,000.
- Keep የ- chains short. "የኢትዮጵያ የሥራና ክህሎት ሚኒስቴር የሠራተኛ ጉዳይ ዳይሬክቶሬት የኃላፊ" is four የ in a row and
  no one reads it; break it with a verb or a comma.
- Passive and impersonal constructions carry news: ተገለጸ, ተጠቅሷል, እየተዘጋጀ ነው. Use them; they are not
  weak writing in Amharic the way they are in English.
- One idea per sentence. Ethiopian news prose is not short, but it is not tangled either.

| በመስመር ላይ | **ኦንላይን** is accepted in a CHAT reply — the word people actually use | 2026-09-20 |
| ሒሳብ (for a bank account) | **አካውንት** is accepted in a CHAT reply | 2026-09-20 |

The last two were approved for Bini's chat answers, where the everyday word wins. Whether an article
should also write ኦንላይን and አካውንት, or the formal ገጽ/ሒሳብ, has not been decided — ask before assuming,
and do not read these two rows as overturning "no chat loan words" above.

ግልጽ, on the other hand, is a spelling rule and holds everywhere: chat, articles, guides, the lot.

## Dates and calendars — the rule that catches everyone

The chat voice file already states this and it applies with more force in print:

- A date from a foreign source or an international story is Gregorian: mark it **እ.ኤ.አ.** and use the
  transliterated month — እ.ኤ.አ. ሴፕቴምበር 20 ቀን 2026.
- A date from an Ethiopian document printed in ዓ.ም. keeps its Ethiopian month and its ዓ.ም. — ነሐሴ 8 ቀን
  2017 ዓ.ም. Never move a day number between calendars, and never write ዓ.ም. after a Gregorian year.
- In a domestic story, giving both is a courtesy the reader notices: "ነሐሴ 8 ቀን 2017 ዓ.ም. (እ.ኤ.አ. ኦገስት
  14 ቀን 2025)".

## What we never do

- Never copy a sentence from ሪፖርተር, አዲስ ዘመን, ኢዜአ, ፋና or anyone else into our article. The patterns
  above are learned from them; the words must be ours. Copying is theft, it is noticed in Addis, and it
  would cost more than any article is worth.
- Never publish a figure, a law number, a hotline or an official's title we cannot source.
- Never write a headline the article does not support.
- Never use a person in trouble as a story without their consent.

## Before publishing — the four checks

1. Read it aloud. Can you hear English underneath? Rewrite.
2. Does every figure name its source in the same sentence?
3. Is every date carrying the right calendar mark?
4. Count the chat loan words: አፕ, ሊንክ, ሳይት, ኦኬ. In an article the count should be zero.

## Internal links — every article, from 22 September 2026

The owner's instruction: every post links back into the site. Not decoration — it is how a reader gets
from a story to the thing that helps them, and how Google learns that our pages belong together. A page
nothing links to is a page nobody arrives at.

**The rule: at least three internal links in every article, and each one has to earn its place.**

- Link where the reader would actually need it. An article about importing a laptop links the customs
  duty guide at the sentence where the cost comes up, not in a list at the bottom.
- Link to a SECTION when the subject has one: /jobs (or a category page like /jobs/category/it),
  /tenders, /news, /guides, /ai, /ride.
- Link to our own earlier article when it genuinely explains the next question. Two AI articles in one
  week should know about each other.
- Amharic anchor text for Amharic readers. "የጉምሩክ ቀረጥና ታክስ መመሪያ", not "click here".
- Never link the same page twice in one article, and never link a page that does not exist - check it.

What NOT to do: a block of unrelated links at the end. Search engines discount it and readers ignore it.
