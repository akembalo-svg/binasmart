# Addis Ababa · አዲስ አበባ — practical knowledge for Bini

Addis Ababa (አዲስ አበባ, "new flower") is the capital of Ethiopia and the seat of the African Union and the UN Economic Commission for Africa. It sits at about 2,300–2,400 metres, which makes it one of the highest capitals in the world: mornings and nights are cool all year, the sun is strong at midday, and newcomers often feel the altitude for a day or two. The long rains (kiremt) run from roughly June to September; the dry season is October to May. Population is in the millions and growing fast; the city is spread out, and traffic is the daily reality that BinaPool exists to soften.

## Sub-cities (ክፍለ ከተማ)

The city is divided into eleven sub-cities: Addis Ketema, Akaki Kality, Arada, Bole, Gullele, Kirkos, Kolfe Keranio, Lideta, Nifas Silk-Lafto, Yeka and Lemi Kura. Each sub-city is split into woredas, and most government one-stop services (Fayda, IDs, business licences) are handled at the woreda or sub-city office. Addresses in everyday speech use landmarks and neighbourhood names, not street numbers.

## Neighbourhoods and what is there

- **Bole (ቦሌ):** the airport district and the main business and hotel area. Bole International Airport with Terminal 2 for international flights; Bole Road (Africa Avenue) with Edna Mall, Friendship Building and Millennium Hall; Bole Medhanialem church and the streets around it, full of cafés and offices. Many embassies, banks and NGOs are here. BinaSmart's Bole corridor ends at Bole Medhanialem.
- **Kazanchis (ካዛንችስ):** the diplomatic and conference quarter around the UN Economic Commission for Africa, with large hotels and ministries; a short walk from Meskel Square and from the Sheraton at the foot of the hill towards Arat Kilo. Office commute destination.
- **Megenagna (መገናኛ):** the eastern transport hub where the ring road, the Bole road and the road to CMC and Ayat meet; a light-rail station, minibus terminals and Zefmesh Grand Mall. Almost every morning commute from the east passes through it, which is why it is BinaPool's main station.
- **Piassa (ፒያሳ):** the old commercial heart of the city in Arada: St George Cathedral, the historic Taitu Hotel, jewellers on Adwa Avenue, and the road down to Merkato. Arat Kilo (አራት ኪሎ) and Sidist Kilo (ስድስት ኪሎ) are the university and ministries area just east of Piassa, with the Addis Ababa University campus, the National Museum (home of Lucy) and the Entoto road climbing north.
- **Mexico (ሜክሲኮ):** the square and roundabout south-west of the centre, a big minibus interchange, near Lideta, the Addis Ababa Stadium and Meskel Square.
- **Meskel Square (መስቀል አደባባይ):** the city's main square and event ground; the Meskel celebration in late September fills it; the light-rail interchange and the road to Bole start here.
- **CMC, Summit and Ayat (ሲኤምሲ · ሰሚት · አያት):** large residential areas in the east along the road out of Megenagna; condominium blocks, new apartment towers and the CMC roundabout. Morning traffic from here into Bole is heavy.
- **Gerji (ገርጂ):** between Megenagna and Bole, residential and offices; on the way in from CMC.
- **Sarbet, Lafto and Jemo (ሳርቤት · ላፍቶ · ጀሞ):** the south-west; Sarbet near the African Union headquarters area, Jemo with its condominium estates further out.
- **Kality and Akaki (ቃሊቲ · አቃቂ):** the south, industrial areas, the dry port, and the Kality bus terminal for buses to the south of the country. Lamberet (ላምበረት), on the eastern ring road, is the terminal for buses east. Autobus Tera, next to Merkato, is the historic intercity bus station.
- **Merkato (መርካቶ):** the huge open-air market west of Piassa in Addis Ketema, one of the largest in Africa; wholesale of almost everything. Crowded; keep phones and bags close.
- **Lideta, Kirkos, Gotera, Saris (ልደታ · ቂርቆስ · ጎተራ · ሳሪስ):** central and south-central districts along the road from Mexico towards Kality.
- **Torhailoch and Kolfe (ጦር ኃይሎች · ኮልፌ):** the western end of the light rail line and the western districts.
- **Entoto (እንጦጦ):** the hills north of the city with the Entoto Park, eucalyptus forests and the historic Entoto Maryam church; the viewpoint over the whole city.
- **Unity Park (አንድነት ፓርክ)** at the Grand Palace, **Friendship Park (ወዳጅነት ፓርክ)** near Meskel Square, and the **Sheger** riverside projects are the newer public parks.

## Getting around

- **Blue-and-white minibus taxis** run fixed routes and are the cheapest way to move; you pay the conductor (ወያላ) in cash. Queues form at hubs like Megenagna, Mexico and Piassa at rush hour.
- **Anbessa City Bus** and the newer **Sheger** buses run longer routes on fixed fares.
- **Light rail (ቀላል ባቡር):** two lines that cross at the centre, one east–west between Ayat and Torhailoch and one north–south between the Menelik II Square area and Kality; the first light rail in sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Ride-hailing:** BinaRide (fixed price, no surge, cash to the driver) and other apps. BinaPool lets four people share one Comfort car on the commute at a fraction of the solo fare.
- **Contract taxis (ኮንትራት):** a car hired for a trip or a daily run, traditionally arranged by phone; BinaPool's daily groups formalise this.
- **Airport:** Bole International is inside the city, roughly 6 km south-east of Meskel Square. International flights use Terminal 2. Bina Airport gives a fixed-price transfer with the flight number tracked.
- **Bajaj (ባጃጅ)** three-wheelers serve the outer districts and side streets where minibuses do not go.

## Daily life facts

- **Currency:** Ethiopian birr (ETB). Cash is still king; **Telebirr** (Ethio Telecom's wallet) and **CBE Birr** are the everyday mobile payments; card payments online go through gateways such as Chapa.
- **Phones:** country code +251; mobile numbers start with 09 (written +251 9…). Ethio Telecom and Safaricom Ethiopia are the networks. Data is metered and often slow, which is why bina.et pages are built light.
- **Emergency numbers:** police 991, ambulance 907, fire 939.
- **Ethiopian time:** many people count hours from sunrise, so "1 o'clock" in the morning is 7:00 by the international clock; when arranging a pickup, confirm which clock is meant. BinaSmart shows both on its programme pages.
- **Calendar:** the Ethiopian calendar has 13 months and runs about seven to eight years behind the Gregorian one; New Year (Enkutatash) falls on 11 September (12 September in a leap year). Big public holidays include Meskel (late September), Genna (Christmas, 7 January), Timket (Epiphany, 19 January), Fasika (Easter), Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, and Adwa Victory Day (2 March).
- **Languages:** Amharic is the working language; Afaan Oromoo, Tigrinya and many others are spoken; English is common in business and on signs.
- **Health:** major public hospitals include Tikur Anbessa (Black Lion) near Lideta, St Paul's Hospital in Gullele, Zewditu Memorial near Kazanchis, Yekatit 12 near Sidist Kilo, Menelik II and the Alert centre; many private hospitals and clinics are in Bole and around the ring road. Pharmacies are everywhere; bring prescriptions.
- **Money and safety:** ATMs of the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia, Awash, Dashen, Abyssinia and others are common in Bole and the centre. Pickpocketing happens in crowds (Merkato, minibus queues); ride-hailing with a known driver, name and plate is the safer choice at night.
- **Weather to plan for:** carry a light jacket even in the dry season; during the rains streets flood and traffic slows, so allow more time and use the fixed-price options that do not surge.

## Landmarks people ask about

National Museum of Ethiopia (Lucy) at Amist Kilo; Ethnological Museum in the old palace at Sidist Kilo; Holy Trinity Cathedral near Arat Kilo; St George Cathedral at Piassa; the Grand Palace and Unity Park; Meskel Square; Entoto hills and park; Merkato; Bole Medhanialem church; the African Union headquarters; the UN Economic Commission for Africa in Kazanchis; Millennium Hall on Bole Road; Addis Ababa Stadium near Mexico; the Science Museum near the palace; Sheger Park.
